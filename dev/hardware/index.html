Package¶

A 10-pin metal-lid LGA, 2.0 mm x 2.0 mm, at most 0.8 mm high (table 3). The vent hole is on the lid; keep it open, and keep vias, traces and solder mask out from under the part (section 8.2).

Never hold SDI, SDO, SCK or CSB high while VDDIO is off: the current through the protection diodes can damage the part (section 4.2).

The chip compensates both values itself (section 4.1): the data registers and the FIFO hold pressure in pascals and temperature in degrees Celsius as fixed-point numbers, and the driver only converts their scale.

Interface selection¶

After a power-on or soft reset the interface is in I2C mode (section 5.1). With CSB tied to VDDIO it stays there. The first CSB low phase with at least 16 SCK edges changes it to SPI until the next reset; that first frame carries no valid data. The SPI port of the driver sends this dummy read itself, in EnsureInitialized() and again after its soft reset, because a soft reset returns the interface to I2C (table 16). An I2C board must never pull CSB low: the chip would leave I2C mode until the next power cycle.

I2C¶

  • Address 0x46 with SDO at VSS, 0x47 with SDO at VDDIO (section 5.6); SDO must not float.
  • 100 kHz to 1 MHz (table 4): fast mode plus needs a bus capacitance under 100 pF.
  • A register read is a write of the address, then a read; the chip keeps the address across a STOP, so a repeated START or a STOP and START both work (figures 15, 16). The driver calls WriteRead().
  • The soft reset (CMD = 0xB6) is not acknowledged (section 7.36); the driver expects the NACK and proves the reset by the reads that follow.

SPI¶

  • Four-wire, mode 0 or 3, the mode chosen by SCK at the CSB falling edge (section 5.5); up to 12 MHz with VDDIO at or above 1.62 V, 7 MHz below (table 4).
  • Bit 7 of the first byte is the read flag, bits 6 to 0 the address; read data starts at the second byte, with no dummy byte (figures 8 to 11).
  • CSB stays high at least 80 ns between frames (table 18). After a write to INT_CONFIG the driver waits 1 µs before it reads it back (section 5.5.4); it never uses hybrid bursts.
  • Three-wire SPI (DRIVE_CONFIG.spi3_en) is not supported: the driver never writes DRIVE_CONFIG.
  • A pull-up on MISO (R1, 47 kΩ, not in figure 25) is recommended. After a reset the chip is back in I2C mode, where SDO is an input, and the first frame only selects SPI (section 5.1): MISO floats. With the pull-up the frame reads 0xFF, which every check refuses; without it the driver still runs the reset check after each status read on SPI (see Contract).
  • The circuit labels SCK and SDI with both hooks, WriteRead() or Transfer(): the diagram tool takes one hook per chip pin for every circuit, and these pins serve both buses. On this circuit they are Transfer(); on the I2C circuit, WriteRead() (and Write() for the writes).

INT pin¶

Push-pull or open drain, active high or low, pulsed (105 µs typical) or latched (sections 4.7.3, table 4); the driver writes Config::int_* to INT_CONFIG and the sources to INT_SOURCE. It never reads the pin: the application may wire it to an interrupt and then call ReadInterruptStatus() or ReadMeasurement(). Unused: tie INT to GND and leave int_pin_enable false, its reset value (section 6.2).

Power¶

State Current (typical, 25 °C) How
Deep standby 0.55 µA After EnsureInitialized() with Config{}, and after Deinitialize()
Standby 1.0 µA Standby with a FIFO, an IIR filter, a data rate of 5 Hz or more, or deep_standby = false
Normal, 1 Hz, low power 1.3 µA LowPowerPreset(): deep standby between the measurements (section 4.3.5)
High resolution, 30 Hz 75 µA HighResolutionPreset() (25 Hz)

Deep standby needs all of: deep_standby true, a data rate under 5 Hz, the FIFO off and both IIR filters bypassed (section 4.3.2); EntersDeepStandby() says whether a configuration meets them. The driver uses the longer start-up time (4 ms) for a forced measurement from deep standby.
